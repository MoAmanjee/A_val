01

Counter

Restaurant ordering · written for Mochachos Potchefstroom CBD

A customer messages the branch. The agent can send the two menu pages, then read an order typed in one go — item, flavour, meal, drink, collection or delivery — and only ask for what was left out. The kitchen screen accepts the ticket and later marks it ready. Both of those steps message the customer.

  • Ja
  • Yebo
  • Sharp

“Yes but make it hot” does not place the order. A change is a change.

  • Prices come from the menu database. The agent does not invent them. A hot 1/4 chicken meal with a Coke is R95.90 on the 2026 board.
  • Collection is 17 Nelson Mandela Drive. Delivery in Potchefstroom uses the street address, and the kitchen sets the fee, including free, before the customer confirms.
  • Pay at the till, or pay the driver. A later pass adds PayFast into the shop’s own account, plus a small order page beside the chat.
  • Ask for a person and the chat is left for staff. A halaal question is handed over too — the menu has no answer on file, so the agent does not guess.
  • Promotions go only to people who messaged in the last day, which is the window WhatsApp allows for a free-form note.

02

AutoQuote

Parts and quoting · for a spares counter with its own stock

A customer messages the business. The agent collects the person and the vehicle. The search then runs against that shop’s inventory and fitment list. A draft quotation lands on the dashboard. Staff review it, edit it, and press Approve & send. Only then does the customer receive the official PDF on WhatsApp.

  • The agent cannot approve, send, price, or discount. Those actions are not available to it.
  • Totals are recalculated on the server. A number typed in the browser does not become the quote.
  • Stock, fitment, and price live in the shop’s database. The model never runs SQL.
  • Every record stays inside that business. A second shop on the same platform cannot see the first shop’s quotes.
  • If a send fails, the quote is not marked sent. If the agent hits a fault, the customer gets a plain reply and the chat is flagged for a person.

03

BookBot

Appointments · salons, barbers, tutors, mechanics

The shop adds services, prices, and opening hours, then gets a booking link for WhatsApp, Instagram, and Facebook. Customers pick a service and a free slot. Two people cannot take the same chair. A WhatsApp reminder goes out about 24 hours before.

  • Built for a 14-day trial, then a monthly fee. The build defaults to R299, and that figure is a setting.
  • Reminders use the WhatsApp Cloud API. Outside the 24-hour chat window they need a template approved in Meta.
  • The clock is South African. The server is set to Johannesburg.
  • Anything that spends money — ads, refunds, payouts — waits in an owner queue and runs only after approval.

Guardrails

The agent stops where a person should start.

  1. Prices are yours

    Menu prices, shelf prices, and service prices are read from your data. The chat does not make up a figure to keep the conversation moving.

  2. Sending is a decision

    A parts quote stays a draft until someone at the counter approves it. A kitchen ticket waits for a clear yes. A spending request waits for the owner.

  3. Silence is a handover

    If the menu, the fitment list, or the diary has no answer, the chat goes to staff. That includes a customer who simply asks for a person.

  4. The money lands with the shop

    On the restaurant build, PayFast is the shop’s own merchant account. Keys are stored encrypted. The public setup page never shows them back.
